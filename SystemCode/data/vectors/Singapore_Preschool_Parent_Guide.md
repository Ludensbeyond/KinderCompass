# Singapore preschool guide for parents

*Choices • Enrolment • Fees and subsidies • Learning and care*

Information checked on 9 October 2026. All amounts are in Singapore dollars. Current 2026 arrangements and announced 2027 changes are identified separately.

A suitable preschool gives your child safe, responsive care and meaningful learning, while fitting your family’s schedule and budget. Start with the hours you need, the journey you can sustain and the teachers who will care for your child. Then compare learning approaches and the total fee after confirmed subsidies.

## 1 Understand the preschool landscape

Preschool in Singapore is not compulsory. It covers the years before Primary 1 and includes infant care, childcare and kindergarten. The Early Childhood Development Agency (ECDA) oversees the sector; the Ministry of Education (MOE) also runs MOE Kindergartens and develops the Nurturing Early Learners curriculum framework.

| **Stage**                | **Usual age or cohort** | **What it means**                                             |
|--------------------------|-------------------------|---------------------------------------------------------------|
| Infant care              | 2 to 18 months          | Care routines, relationships, movement and early exploration. |
| Playgroup                | From 18 months          | Early childcare stage; naming and class transitions vary.     |
| Nursery 1 or pre nursery | Turns 3 during the year | Communication, play, routines and growing independence.       |
| Nursery 2 or nursery     | Turns 4 during the year | Broader language, social and early learning experiences.      |
| Kindergarten 1           | Turns 5 during the year | K1; offered by childcare centres and kindergartens.           |
| Kindergarten 2           | Turns 6 during the year | K2; final preschool year before the usual P1 transition.      |

Age labels describe typical cohorts. Confirm the centre’s actual entry criteria and transition date, especially around 18 months or an unusual birth-date boundary.

### Three separate decisions

Service type determines care hours. Operator type affects fee caps and the funding arrangement. Educational approach describes how children learn. A private operator may offer full-day childcare and a Montessori-inspired curriculum; a government-supported operator may also use inquiry and purposeful play.

Use this guide in order, or go straight to section 4 for fees, section 5 for subsidies, section 7 for 2027 changes, section 8 for enrolment or section 9 for the visit checklist.

## 2 Choose the service and operator

| **Option**                  | **What to expect**                                                           | **Check before choosing**                                               |
|-----------------------------|------------------------------------------------------------------------------|-------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Infant care centre          | Centre-based care for babies from 2 months.                                  | Feeding, sleep, caregiver continuity and transition to childcare.       |
| Childcare centre            | Care and education from about 18 months; programmes can continue through K2. | Full-day, half-day or flexible hours; holiday and closure arrangements. |
| Kindergarten                | Shorter educational sessions; care beyond class is a separate question.      | Age levels offered, term calendar and transport or caregiver needs.     |
| MOE Kindergarten with KCare | Four-hour K1/K2 education plus optional extended care.                       | Apply for both services; compare both fees and subsidy routes.          |

### Anchor Operators and Partner Operators

Anchor Operators (AOPs) receive government funding tied to affordability and quality commitments. The five are PCF Sparkletots, My First Skool, M.Y World, Skool4Kidz and E-Bridge. Partner Operator (POP) funding applies to participating centres. Check the exact branch and programme; a brand name alone does not establish the fee cap.

These schemes support operators. Parents benefit through capped fees and can separately qualify for child-level subsidies. AOP or POP status does not describe one teaching philosophy, nor does it replace a visit to the centre.

### MOE Kindergarten and Early Years Centres

MOE Kindergarten (MK) serves Singapore Citizen and Permanent Resident children in K1 and K2. KCare is available at all MKs for families needing a full-day service, operating on weekdays until 7pm, including school holidays, subject to closure days and enrolment arrangements.

In the MK–Early Years Centre partnership, eligible children at participating E-Bridge, My First Skool and PCF EYCs have a guaranteed place at a partner MK. Verify the precise EYC, eligibility and partner MK. The arrangement does not apply automatically to every branch, and a place in MK is distinct from a KCare place.

### Private and other alternatives

Other private, international and faith-based providers can offer different languages, calendars, facilities and educational approaches. Ask about the specific programme’s registration, admissions and funding eligibility. Eligible citizen children in licensed private childcare can receive childcare subsidies; private kindergarten programmes do not qualify for KiFAS.

For babies, ECDA’s Childminding Pilot adds a home-based care option through appointed operators. Its arrangements differ from centre-based infant care. Family care or a nanny may also meet care needs; enrichment classes serve a different purpose from a daily preschool programme.

## 3 Understand learning approaches

Look for a balanced day with conversation, stories, exploration, movement, creative work, social interaction and rest where appropriate. Ask a teacher to explain what children are learning during an ordinary activity. A curriculum label becomes useful when you can see how it changes daily teaching.

### Singapore curriculum frameworks

ECDA’s Early Years Development Framework guides work with children from birth to age 3. MOE’s Nurturing Early Learners framework guides ages 4 to 6. Together they support holistic development rather than a single national preschool textbook. Literacy and numeracy sit alongside physical, creative, social and emotional learning. MOE Kindergarten uses active learning and purposeful play.

| **Approach**                 | **Typical emphasis**                                                                                         | **Useful question for a visit**                                                           |
|------------------------------|--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Play based and inquiry based | Teachers build learning around play, questions, exploration and interaction.                                 | How do you extend a child’s play into language, maths or problem-solving?                 |
| Montessori                   | Prepared materials, practical life, independence, observation and self-directed work; mixed ages are common. | What Montessori training do teachers have, and how are materials and work periods used?   |
| Reggio inspired              | Children’s ideas, collaborative projects, expressive materials and documentation of learning.                | Can you show a project that grew from children’s questions?                               |
| IB Primary Years Programme   | Inquiry across subject boundaries, with play central in the early years.                                     | What is the school’s IB authorisation status and how does the early-years programme work? |
| Waldorf or Steiner inspired  | Rhythm, imitation, stories, imaginative play and practical activities.                                       | How are language, early numeracy and the eventual primary transition supported?           |
| More explicitly structured   | More teacher-led instruction and planned practice; the balance varies by centre.                             | How much of the day involves seatwork, and how is teaching adjusted for readiness?        |

### Language and family values

For any bilingual claim, ask which languages children hear, who teaches them and how often they use them in conversation. Check the actual Mother Tongue options rather than assuming every branch offers the same choices. If faith or cultural practices matter to your family, ask about observances, food and participation expectations.

### How to judge the claims

Ask to see an ordinary timetable, children’s work over time and a sample progress report. Discuss how the centre helps a child who is quiet, very active or unfamiliar with the teaching language. There is no single approach that is automatically best for every child; assess its implementation and fit. Paid enrichment is a separate purchasing decision, not proof of a stronger core programme.

## 4 Build a realistic preschool budget

Compare the same service, citizenship category and fee year. A monthly price may exclude GST, optional classes and transport. Request a written schedule of fees and an estimate that separates confirmed subsidies from assumptions.

| **2026 programme**       | **Monthly fee or cap**   | **Basis**                                |
|--------------------------|--------------------------|------------------------------------------|
| AOP full-day childcare   | $610                    | Before GST and subsidies                 |
| POP full-day childcare   | $650                    | Before GST and subsidies                 |
| AOP full-day infant care | $1,235                  | Before GST and subsidies                 |
| POP full-day infant care | $1,290                  | Before GST and subsidies                 |
| AOP kindergarten         | $150                    | Before GST and KiFAS                     |
| MOE Kindergarten         | $160 citizen / $320 PR | School fee before KiFAS                  |
| KCare at MK              | Up to $425              | Separate 2026 fee before KCare subsidies |

The AOP and POP caps shown apply to Singapore Citizen children. Ask for the relevant quote for a PR or foreign child. Other private providers set their own fees. MK and KCare fees remain payable during the June and December holidays. Confirm the selected centre’s actual KCare charge.

### Illustration using Basic Subsidy only

Assume a citizen child, an eligible working main applicant, a GST-registered centre charging the cap, and no Additional Subsidy. At 9% GST, AOP childcare is $610 × 1.09 − $300 = $364.90 monthly. POP childcare is $650 × 1.09 − $300 = $408.50. These are calculations for programme fees, excluding extras, rather than individual subsidy approvals.

### Costs that change the comparison

- Upfront: registration or administration fee, refundable deposit, uniforms, bedding and required supplies.

- Recurring: programme fee, GST where charged, transport, meals if separate, and optional enrichment.

- Occasional: excursions, photographs, events or materials. Ask what is optional and whether an alternative is provided.

- Exit and disruption: notice period, deposit refund rules, fee increases, closure days and backup care. Check whether fees continue during absence.

A useful first-year estimate is twelve months of net recurring fees, plus non-refundable setup costs and likely extras. Track the refundable deposit separately as cash you need at the start. Add the cost and time of the daily journey. If assistance is still being assessed, ask how the first invoice and any later adjustments will be handled.

## 5 Understand infant and childcare subsidies

Basic and Additional Subsidies support Singapore Citizen children in ECDA-licensed infant care and childcare. They can apply at eligible private centres as well as AOP and POP centres. A citizen parent alone does not establish eligibility if the child is not a citizen.

### Basic Subsidy and the working applicant rule

For full-day care, the working-main-applicant Basic Subsidy is $600 a month for infant care or $300 for childcare. The main applicant is normally the mother, or a single father in the relevant circumstances, working at least 56 hours monthly; part-time and freelance work can count. Do not assume a father’s employment automatically meets this rule for a two-parent household.

The ordinary non-working rate is $150 monthly. There is an important childcare exception: citizen children in families with household income up to $6,000, or qualifying per capita income up to $1,500, can receive the higher Basic Subsidy and means-tested Additional Subsidy regardless of the main applicant’s employment. This is not a blanket infant-care exemption. Other circumstances can be assessed under Special Approval.

### Additional Subsidy in 2026

The usual income ceiling is gross monthly household income (HHI) of $12,000, or per capita income (PCI) of $3,000 for eligible households with at least five family members. Approval uses verified income and household composition. Gross income is not take-home pay; bonuses and other earnings can count, and personal spending is not deducted.

| **Monthly HHI in 2026** | **Childcare maximum AS** | **Infant care maximum AS** |
|-------------------------|--------------------------|----------------------------|
| $3,000 or less         | $467                    | $710                      |
| $3,001–$4,500         | $440                    | $640                      |
| $4,501–$6,000         | $340                    | $500                      |
| $6,001–$7,500         | $260                    | $380                      |
| $7,501–$9,000         | $190                    | $240                      |
| $9,001–$10,500        | $130                    | $100                      |
| $10,501–$12,000       | $80                     | $40                       |

AS means Additional Subsidy. These full-day maximums sit on top of eligible Basic Subsidy. Actual awards depend on the programme fee and required minimum co-payment. Above the HHI ceiling, check PCI eligibility where applicable. Half-day and flexible programmes use different tables.

### Why the maximum may differ from your award

If your fee is already low, subtracting the published maximums can overstate your support. For example, using a $664.90 fee and $300 Basic Subsidy, only $364.90 remains before Additional Subsidy; the minimum co-payment still has to be respected. Obtain the actual net payable amount from the centre after assessment.

## 6 Other grants and financial support

### Kindergarten Fee Assistance Scheme

KiFAS is for eligible citizen children in MOE and AOP kindergartens. It is means-tested and does not depend on the parent being employed. The 2026 income ceiling is $12,000 HHI or $3,000 PCI for eligible larger households. It does not apply to private kindergartens. The actual award depends on the fee and minimum co-payment, so a low fee can limit the subsidy paid.

Apply through the kindergarten. Eligible low-income families can also seek the annual KiFAS Start-Up Grant, capped at $240, for approved initial costs. Its income criteria are stricter than KiFAS: currently HHI up to $1,900 or qualifying PCI up to $650, with assessment of the application.

### KCare has a separate subsidy route

A citizen child enrolled in KCare can receive a $150 monthly Basic Subsidy. Eligible families can receive means-tested Additional Subsidy; lower-income families with HHI up to $6,000 can qualify regardless of the applicant’s employment. Ask MK to check your circumstances using the KCare rules. KiFAS reduces the MK education fee, while KCare subsidies reduce the separate care fee; do not apply the ordinary $300 childcare Basic Subsidy to KCare.

### When the remaining bill is still difficult

Ask the centre about Child Care Financial Assistance and the childcare Start-Up Grant if your family faces financial hardship after subsidies. These involve assessment and are not automatic grants for every child. Special Approval can also help when the applicant cannot meet the work requirement because of circumstances such as job-seeking, illness, study or caregiving. Ask what evidence and renewal dates apply.

KidSTART supports eligible lower-income families with young children through help with early childhood development and caregiving. It is broader family support, rather than a standard monthly fee rebate. A preschool or Social Service Office can help identify other suitable assistance.

### Using the Child Development Account

The Child Development Account (CDA) is a restricted savings account that can pay approved education and healthcare expenses at Baby Bonus Approved Institutions. Confirm the preschool’s status and which charges it accepts. CDA payments reduce your bank cash outlay, but they still use your child’s funds; they are distinct from a fee subsidy.

Under current Baby Bonus arrangements, the CDA First Step Grant and government co-matching help build the balance. For eligible children born from 18 February 2025, the First Step Grant is $5,000 for the first or second child and $10,000 for the third or subsequent child. Older birth cohorts have different entitlements. Co-matching requires deposits and is capped. Read the 2027 transition in section 7 before making a longer-term savings plan.

## 7 Changes to plan for in 2027

The arrangements below have been announced as at 9 October 2026. Keep their effective dates separate from the fees and assistance applying to a child attending preschool now.

### From January 2027 wider subsidy eligibility

The income ceiling for means-tested infant and childcare Additional Subsidy and KiFAS rises to $15,000 HHI or $3,400 PCI, subject to the relevant household and scheme conditions. More income bands also shift; an existing recipient may qualify for a different amount even without an income change.

| **2027 monthly HHI** | **Full-day childcare maximum AS** | **Full-day infant care maximum AS** |
|----------------------|-----------------------------------|-------------------------------------|
| $3,500 or less      | $467                             | $710                               |
| $3,501–$5,500      | $440                             | $640                               |
| $5,501–$7,500      | $340                             | $500                               |
| $7,501–$9,000      | $260                             | $380                               |
| $9,001–$11,000     | $190                             | $240                               |
| $11,001–$13,000    | $130                             | $100                               |
| $13,001–$15,000    | $80                              | $40                                |

These are full-day Additional Subsidy maximums, subject to eligibility, actual fees and minimum co-payments. They are not total subsidies or guaranteed payouts. Use the official table for PCI bands and other service types. Confirm how your centre will handle the transition and any required reassessment.

### From April 2027 the SG Child Support Package

The announced SG Child Support Package replaces Baby Bonus and the Large Families Scheme, with payouts starting in April 2027. Existing Baby Bonus children transition automatically. Eligible children turning 1 to 16 from 2026 onwards receive $2,000 in annual Child Credits, with the first payout from April 2027.

For citizen children born from 1 April 2027, the package includes a $10,000 Baby Gift, a $5,000 CDA First Step Grant and a $5,000 CDA co-matching cap. Earlier birth cohorts have transition arrangements. Existing CDA co-matching caps can be used until 30 September 2027, with caps adjusted to $5,000 from 1 October 2027. Check your child’s cohort-specific entitlement before depositing.

The official guidance asks parents to set up an eligible account linked to their child’s PayNow ID before 1 April 2027. The CDA is not that PayNow payout account. These family benefits are separate from monthly preschool subsidies.

### What parents should do

Get a 2027 fee quote, check both income measures where eligible, and ask which subsidy steps the centre will complete. For CDA and family benefits, use the child’s birth cohort rather than an old generic benefits chart. Retain the approval notices and compare them with the first new-year invoice.

## 8 Enrolment from search to first day

### Step 1 Set your family requirements

Write down the child’s start date, required drop-off and collection times, affordable net monthly amount, travel limit, languages and any care or developmental needs. Search early if you have a fixed return-to-work date; waiting lists and intake practices vary by centre. There is no universal deadline for every private childcare programme.

### Step 2 Shortlist and confirm availability

Use LifeSG Preschool Search to compare locations, services and centre details. Contact centres directly to confirm the vacancy for your child’s age and intended start month. Ask how the waitlist works, whether a fee is payable and what turns an expression of interest into a confirmed place. Keep a workable backup option.

### Step 3 Visit and read the agreement

Meet the centre leader and, if possible, the likely class teacher. Observe an ordinary session. Request the handbook, fee schedule, closure calendar, withdrawal policy and subsidy estimate. For an infant, discuss the future childcare place; for an EYC, check the partner MK transition. Get material promises in writing before paying.

### Step 4 Accept the place and prepare documents

- Child identification and citizenship or immigration documents, plus parent or guardian identification.

- Contact and address details, emergency contacts and authorised collection arrangements.

- Immunisation records, allergy or medical information, and relevant care instructions.

- Income, work and household information needed for subsidy assessment; additional evidence for special circumstances.

- Payment arrangements, enrolment agreement and any requested consent forms.

This is a preparation checklist, not a universal document list. Use the centre’s current requirements. Singpass and Myinfo can support the government application process, but incomplete or incorrect records may need follow-up.

### Step 5 Complete the subsidy application

After the preschool place is confirmed, complete the enrolment and subsidy form through the centre’s process. LifeSG’s Form 1 is used for preschool enrolment details and subsidy applications. Check both parents’ required information and consents. Ask for the outcome, effective month and net bill. If household circumstances change, request reassessment through the preschool.

### The separate MOE Kindergarten route

MK runs an annual K1 registration exercise for the following year, with published eligibility, priority and balloting rules. The 2027 K1 online exercise ran from 23 to 31 March 2026. As that window has passed, contact the preferred MK about vacancies or its waitlist. K2 admission depends on available places. Indicate your KCare need and complete its separate enrolment requirements.

Admission priority involves factors such as citizenship, income, siblings and distance. Check MOE’s current rules and dates for the intended intake; do not assume that being first to submit guarantees admission.

## 9 Assess quality and safety during a visit

Watch how adults respond when a child is upset, needs help or disagrees with another child. Ask how staff are supported and how families hear about concerns. Attractive rooms and a strong brand are useful context, but the daily interactions deserve close attention.

### Understand staffing numbers

ECDA publishes minimum qualified programme staff-to-child ratios. The ordinary ratios and permitted arrangements with an additional assistant educator are shown below. They are regulatory staffing arrangements, not a promise of small class size or individual attention.

| **Group**         | **Ordinary ratio** | **With assistant educator**   |
|-------------------|--------------------|-------------------------------|
| Infant            | 1:5                | Not applicable                |
| Playgroup         | 1:8                | 1 educator + 1 assistant : 12 |
| Pre nursery or N1 | 1:12               | 1 educator + 1 assistant : 18 |
| Nursery or N2     | 1:15               | 1 educator + 1 assistant : 20 |
| K1                | 1:20               | 1 educator + 1 assistant : 25 |
| K2                | 1:25               | 1 educator + 1 assistant : 30 |

Ask about the actual number of children and qualified adults in your child’s room, including during breaks, opening hours and staff absence. Ask how often teachers have changed and how new staff learn individual care routines.

### Questions worth asking

- Relationships: How do you comfort a child? How do you guide biting, hitting or refusal without humiliation?

- Care: How do feeding, toileting, sleep and outdoor activities work? What happens if a child needs extra help?

- Safety: Who can enter or collect a child? How are allergies, medication, excursions and emergencies managed?

- Communication: When are incidents reported? Who follows up, and how are concerns escalated?

- Learning: How do you record progress and adapt activities? Can we see a typical day and a sample portfolio?

### Licensing and SPARK

Check the centre’s current operating status and whether it holds SPARK recognition. SPARK is ECDA’s quality framework, with SPARK 2.0 focusing on self-appraisal and validated quality improvement. It is separate from basic licensing and does not remove the need to assess the particular classroom.

### CCTV and handling concerns

CCTV is not an unrestricted parent livestream. For a safety-related incident involving your child, ECDA advises a written request to the preschool, which assesses the request; access can be restricted during an official investigation. Document concerns with dates and facts, contact the centre leader and operator, and escalate unresolved regulatory or safety concerns to ECDA. Seek urgent assistance for an immediate danger.

## 10 Support health development and settling in

### Share needs early and agree on a plan

Tell the preschool about allergies, medication, feeding or sleep difficulties, disability, developmental concerns and any professional advice already received. Ask who will be responsible for the plan, what the centre can provide and when you will review progress together. An inclusive mission statement should translate into specific support for your child.

### Where developmental support can begin

If you are concerned about communication, movement, hearing, behaviour or learning, discuss concrete observations with the teacher and a qualified healthcare professional. Ask a polyclinic, GP or paediatrician about assessment and referral. A preschool can observe a child and support learning, but its marketing label is not a clinical assessment.

| **Support**             | **General role**                                                                                               | **Parent action**                                                                                              |
|-------------------------|----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| DS-LS                   | Development Support and Learning Support for K1/K2 children needing low levels of early intervention.          | Ask whether the preschool offers it and how children are identified and referred.                              |
| EIPIC and EIPIC-P       | Early intervention services for eligible children with developmental needs; programme suitability is assessed. | Discuss referral with the healthcare team and SG Enable; ask about fees, subsidies and waiting time.           |
| Other preschool support | Selected arrangements, such as DS-Plus or the Inclusive Support Programme, serve particular needs.             | Ask which options are appropriate and locally available; avoid assuming every centre provides every programme. |

Do not assume that enrolment in preschool automatically includes therapy or that different funded intervention schemes can be used together. Check each programme’s eligibility, referral requirements and rules on concurrent enrolment. Coordinate preschool, therapy, transport and rest as one weekly schedule.

### Health and care arrangements

Have immunisation records ready. Singapore requires childhood vaccination against measles and diphtheria, subject to applicable medical provisions. Ask your healthcare provider about missing records or doses. Request the centre’s illness and return-to-school policy, medication procedures and approach to food allergies. Arrange a backup caregiver for illness and planned closures.

### A practical settling in plan

- Before starting: visit if possible, practise the journey and share your child’s comfort strategies and daily routine.

- At the start: agree on a predictable goodbye and the centre’s transition arrangements. Ask who will comfort your child and how updates will be shared.

- Over the following weeks: review eating, rest, participation and relationships with the teacher. Compare observations rather than relying on the amount of crying at drop-off alone.

- If concerns persist: ask for a specific support plan and a review date. Seek appropriate professional advice when wellbeing or development is a concern.

## 11 Prepare for Primary 1 and make a choice

### Readiness is broader than early academics

Alongside early literacy and numeracy, help your child practise asking for help, following routines, managing belongings, taking turns and recovering from small disappointments. Reading together, talking about daily experiences, playing with quantities and enjoying movement are practical ways to support learning at home. Coordinate with the teacher on a few useful goals rather than buying enrichment by default.

Ask the K2 teacher how they support transition and communicate learning or support needs. Rehearse practical tasks such as opening a lunch container and recognising belongings. Any child with additional needs may benefit from earlier planning between the family, preschool, receiving school and relevant professionals.

### Preschool admission and primary admission are separate

Children from an MK can be eligible for Phase 2A registration at the primary school where that MK is located. This is not a guaranteed P1 place: oversubscribed phases can require balloting. Families still need to complete the P1 registration process and meet that exercise’s requirements. Attending an unrelated preschool near a desired primary school does not itself create the MK priority.

MOE has announced changes from the 2027 P1 registration exercise, for children entering in 2028; check the rules for your child’s actual intake. For an international school pathway, check that school’s admissions and support arrangements. PR and foreign families should use the rules applicable to their child’s status.

### A shortlist that is easy to compare

| **Criterion**      | **What to record for each centre**                                      |
|--------------------|-------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Care and logistics | Confirmed hours, travel time, closure days and backup-care needs.       |
| People and safety  | Teacher interactions, staffing stability and response to concerns.      |
| Child fit          | Language options, support needs, daily routine and learning approach.   |
| Affordability      | Net fees after assessed support, extras, deposit and annual commitment. |
| Certainty          | Confirmed vacancy and start date, written terms and future transition.  |

First remove centres that cannot meet a non-negotiable need. Then compare the remaining options using the same evidence. A nearby centre with responsive teachers and manageable fees can be a better fit than a prestigious option that creates daily stress. This is a practical decision method, not a ranking of preschool brands.

### Questions parents commonly ask

Must my child attend nursery before K1? Prior nursery attendance is not a universal requirement; check admissions and discuss the child’s transition needs. Does a higher fee ensure better quality? It does not establish teacher responsiveness or fit. Can I change centres? Usually yes, subject to availability and contract terms; arrange the subsidy update and avoid an unintended gap. Is a waitlist a place? No; obtain written confirmation of an offer and start date.

Before accepting, confirm five things: the place, the daily care plan, the full cost, the assistance application and the exit terms. Keep the centre’s written answers with your enrolment documents.
